Studies

I did my formal studies at the University of California, Berkeley, where they have an incredible Cognitive Science program — an interdisciplinary degree spanning six different fields: psychology, neuroscience, philosophy, anthropology, linguistics, and computer science. Essentially, the degree explores intelligence and the mind from each of these six perspectives:

  • Psychology: What is the mind from a behavioral perspective?
  • Neuroscience: What is the mind from a chemical and biological perspective? Simply, its biological substrate — the brain.
  • Philosophy: How are the mind and brain connected? What does it mean to have intelligence? To what extent can you have one (brain, mind, intelligence) and not another? You learn to ask questions that may not have answers, and to build logical proofs.
  • Anthropology: How did our brains evolve? How did the mind evolve? Did they evolve together? Could the brain have evolved as it did without consciousness ever emerging? How have different attributes — physical, cultural, biological, chemical — evolved differently in different places? And what does the future hold, now that we’re evolving our minds much faster than our bodies can physically keep up?
  • Linguistics: We are “a symbolic species” — while language in some form isn’t unique to humans, the way we use it might be. Or, seen through another lens, language can seem like something almost distinct from humans altogether, something evolving on its own — maybe instead of humans evolving and then using language, we evolved biologically for language.
  • Computer Science: And how can we model all of this — the mind, the brain, language, emotions, choices, evolution? You learn the basics of programming, logic, and simulation.
